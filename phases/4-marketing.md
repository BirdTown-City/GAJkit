# Phase 4: How we market the candidate

**What it gives the candidate:** a clear, honest choice of which version of themselves to present and to whom, a short list of people to talk to first, a base resume, and a LinkedIn profile that lets recruiters find and understand them. They go into conversations knowing what they stand for.

**Before you start:** the candidate has authorized a direction and next-role objectives (Phase 3).

## Rules for this phase

- **Honest marketing.** Positioning chooses emphasis among approved, evidenced claims. It never invents a claim, a persona or a scope. The rules in `START-HERE.md` apply in full.
- **People first.** Most real conversations, and most offers, come through people the candidate already knows and recruiters who find them, not through cold applications. Relationships come first, recruiters second, cold applications third and only if the candidate wants them.
- **No outreach without the candidate.** Naming a person, or planning a conversation, doesn't authorize contacting them. The candidate approves each message and its wording.

## Step 1. Choose the positioning

Propose, and have the candidate decide:

- **who we're trying to attract**: the employers, functions and people most likely to hire for this direction;
- **which roles they stand for**: normally one or two role families;
- **which two or three stories lead**, and the one thing each should make people remember;
- **one plain sentence** on what they do, through the problems they solve and what they're good at, not a list of titles;
- **what not to claim**.

Emphasis may shift by audience, but never into a persona the evidence doesn't support. Write the approved positioning in `candidate-profile.md`.

## Step 2. Put relationships first

Build the people list with the candidate:

- start from the people named in Phase 2 and their existing relationships: former colleagues, managers, clients, vendors, partners, alumni and friends in the field;
- pick a short first list of people worth a conversation now, and why each one;
- for each, record the relationship, the purpose of the conversation and a next action with a date;
- include any recruiters already in touch, and agree how inbound recruiter messages will be answered (Step 5);
- agree how the list stays current: every conversation gets a last-contact date, an outcome, and a next action and date, or a clear close.

Write it in the People section of `tracker.md`. If the tracker doesn't exist yet, create it from the layout in `phases/5-search-and-applying.md`; the People part is repeated below.

Rules:

- Confirm each person's current role and employer before relying on it.
- Draft messages from the positioning. The candidate approves and sends them.
- A ready application is never held back while waiting on a warm path.

## Step 3. Set up the base resume

The base resume is the one approved resume that every tailored version starts from. Tailoring changes a copy of it; it never starts from a blank page.

1. Start from the candidate's best current resume, if they have one.
2. Check every claim against the Story Bank and the rules in `START-HERE.md`. Use the resume bank's approved wording where it fits.
3. Shape it to the positioning: the lead stories carry the most weight.
4. With the candidate, mark each section:
   - **Fixed**: identity and history, such as names, employers, titles and dates. These never change without the candidate's approval.
   - **Default**: structure and content that carry forward unless an edit list changes them.
   - **Tailored**: what may change for each role, such as the headline, summary, which bullets lead, and technical keywords.
5. Keep the layout simple enough for screening software (applicant tracking systems) to read: standard headings, real employer, title and date lines, no text in images or tables that confuse parsers. If the candidate can, test it once in a real application form.
6. Agree the house style: fonts, spacing, page length and file naming.
7. The candidate approves it.

Record it in the Base resume section of `candidate-profile.md`. Only change the base on purpose, with the candidate's approval, usually through Step 9 of the resume build in Phase 5.

### What makes a good resume

- **One hiring argument, not a bag of bullets.** The resume says one clear thing about who the candidate is for this kind of role, why to believe it, and the evidence that proves it.
- **Seniority before keywords.** A bullet opens with what the candidate owned, its scope or its outcome, then the method, then the vocabulary.
- **Strong verb, precise object, material scope, result where supported.** One main point per bullet.
- **Show the capability through evidence**, rather than naming it ("strategic leader").
- **Use numbers when they show scale or outcome**, keep their qualifiers, and leave out true numbers that make big work sound small. Don't force a metric.
- **Specifics earn their space** only when they add credibility, scale or difference. Don't squeeze an interview story into a bullet.
- **Natural language** the candidate could say and defend out loud.
- **When it's too long**, tighten wording, then cut lower-value evidence, then combine. Change fonts or margins only with the candidate's say-so.

## Step 4. LinkedIn

LinkedIn expresses the positioning; it doesn't define it.

**If the candidate has no profile**, draft a complete first profile from the approved evidence and the positioning. The candidate creates the account, chooses its settings, and enters or approves every field. Then check the live profile against the draft.

**If they have one:**

1. **Capture it.** Ask for the current text, an export or screenshots of the headline, About, experience, skills, Featured, and job preferences or "Open to work", where relevant. Date the capture.
2. **Check platform guidance when it matters.** If a change depends on how LinkedIn search, skills, field limits or "Open to work" behave, check current LinkedIn guidance rather than relying on memory.
3. **Audit each field** against the evidence and positioning: accuracy, consistency of titles and dates, seniority, stale or contradictory wording, unsupported claims, overloading, and accurate search terms that are missing where they matter. Mark each: **KEEP**, **CORRECT**, **STRENGTHEN**, **DE-EMPHASIZE**, **ADD**, **REMOVE**, or **NEEDS THE CANDIDATE'S DECISION**.
4. **Propose a short change set**, in this priority order: factual errors; contradictions in professional identity; missing high-value proof or unclear wording; search and findability; optional polish. For each change: the field, current text, proposed text and why. Don't stuff keywords, don't turn About into a prose resume, and don't make the headline carry every capability.
5. **The candidate decides** each change that affects positioning or anything they'd disclose publicly. Plain factual corrections don't need a debate.
6. **The candidate applies the changes**, unless you have access and they've asked you to.
7. **Read it back.** Check the live profile against the approved changes. A change isn't done until it shows on the profile.

Preserve strong existing wording when it's still accurate. If the candidate wants to put this off, note it and why in the journal.

## Step 5. Answering recruiters who reach out

When a recruiter or hiring contact reaches out, by LinkedIn or anywhere else:

1. Add them to People in `tracker.md`, with origin **RECRUITER-INBOUND**, the role or purpose, and a next action and date.
2. Check the role quickly against the candidate's hard constraints and authorized direction.
3. Draft a short, prompt reply from the positioning: interest or a polite no, what the candidate is looking for, and a next step.
4. If the role is real, add it to Opportunities in `tracker.md` with the same origin.

The candidate approves and sends each reply. A polite no keeps the relationship: record a clear close or a future follow-up. Don't overstate interest or fit.

## Step 6. Check they got there

Ask, briefly:

- In a sentence, what do you do and what are you looking for?
- Which two or three stories will you lead with?
- Who are the first people you'll talk to, and why them?

**Move on** when they can answer these. Relationship work, recruiter replies and LinkedIn carry on from here inside Phase 5.
