# Phase 5: Search and applying

**What it gives the candidate:** a steady routine using the channels they choose, initially considering relationships, recruiters and optional cold applications, then testing that order against their actual results. Each application they send is honest, tailored and worth sending.

**Before you start:** Phase 4 has agreed the channels to use and prepared the supported positioning or base resume the next activity needs. Relationship follow-ups and recruiter replies can start before the rest of Phase 4 is finished; a people list is needed only when relationship work is chosen.

This phase is ongoing. Do not mark it complete because a shortlist, resume or daily run exists. Earlier discovery can remain open under the provisional rule in `START-HERE.md`; perform only work whose specific evidence and candidate decisions are ready. An application still needs its supported base resume, five checks and candidate review. No suitable contacts is not a block: respect the candidate's channel choices.

## How the search is organized

### Channel order

1. **Relationships**: existing relationships, referrals and warm introductions.
2. **Recruiters**: answering inbound recruiters well (Phase 4, Step 5) and staying easy to find.
3. **Cold applications**: finding postings and applying with no one behind them. This is a second channel, and optional. Treat it as an experiment to measure, not the default way to spend the candidate's time.

This order is a working rule, not a law. The regular review (below) tests it for this candidate, and the candidate may change it.

### Cold discovery: OFF until the candidate turns it on

The Settings section of `candidate-profile.md` holds one switch: cold discovery ON or OFF. It starts OFF. While it's OFF, the daily run skips job-alert triage, routine searching for postings, and checking a promising employer for other openings. Record each as "skipped: cold discovery off". A skipped step isn't an empty or failed search and is never reported as one. An explicit request from the candidate still runs a search, and a ready application still goes ahead.

### Where each lead came from

Every person and opportunity in the tracker records its **origin**:

- **RELATIONSHIP**: an existing relationship, referral or warm introduction;
- **RECRUITER-INBOUND**: a recruiter or hiring contact who reached out;
- **COLD**: found through a posting, alert or search, with no one behind it;
- **OTHER** or **UNKNOWN**.

A warm path found later doesn't change an opportunity's origin. The origin is how the regular review shows which channel works.

## The daily run

Run it on the rhythm the candidate wants, such as each weekday morning, or when they ask. If you can run scheduled tasks and the candidate wants it, offer to run it on a schedule.

1. **People and recruiters first.** Read People in `tracker.md`. List the follow-ups due today, any recruiter messages waiting for a reply, and any conversation that has gone quiet and needs a nudge or a close. Draft the messages; the candidate sends them.
2. **Check application results.** If you can read the candidate's email, look for replies about applications: interview requests, assessments, rejections, closures. Otherwise, ask the candidate what's come in. Ignore routine acknowledgements. Update Applications in `tracker.md`. Never infer a rejection from silence.
3. **Recheck the queue.** For each opportunity you're about to spend effort on, open the posting and update its status (below). Mark it CLOSED only when the employer's own site or a message from the employer shows it's closed. If you can't confirm it either way, mark it UNRESOLVED or OFFICIAL, APPLY NOT CHECKED, keep it in the queue, and say what's left to check.
4. **Triage job alerts** (cold discovery ON only). Treat alerts as leads. Drop what's already tracked; evaluate the rest.
5. **Search for new roles** (cold discovery ON only). Search the role families and cues in the authorized direction. Evaluate each plausible role (below). When an employer shows interest or yields one strong role, look at its other current openings.
6. **Decide and rank.** Give each evaluated role a decision, and rank the actionable ones (below).
7. **Pick what to apply to.** With the candidate, choose the roles to build resumes for. Offer to run the resume builds overnight if you can schedule tasks (see "Running the build overnight").
8. **Update the files.** Update `tracker.md` and write a journal entry.
9. **Tell the candidate** in a short update, in the form they prefer: what needs them today, what's ready to review, and what changed.

### Searching around a trigger (optional)

Sometimes an event at an employer, such as an acquisition, leadership change, new regulation, platform migration, new site or restructuring, suggests they'll need someone like the candidate. To follow one up:

1. Confirm the event from the employer or reputable current news.
2. Do one focused search of that employer's openings, using two or three query angles tied to the event.
3. Either a role goes into normal evaluation, or record "trigger, no viable role" and stop.

Don't keep searching an employer to force a trigger to produce a job. Go deeper only when the value is unusually high, the employer's openings are hard to see, or the candidate asks.

## Reading a posting and evaluating a role

Evaluate a role by the work the employer is actually buying, not by its title.

### Reading a posting

Sort each material statement in the posting and application form into:

- **REQUIRED**: stated minimums, credentials, years and capabilities, even without the word "required";
- **PREFERRED**: nice-to-haves;
- **RESPONSIBILITY**: what the person will do;
- **PRACTICAL CONDITION**: location, schedule, travel, pay, employment type and the like.

A sentence can be more than one type. Classify by meaning, not by heading: an explicit prior-experience requirement is a requirement even inside a responsibilities list, and a future duty on its own doesn't create a requirement for past experience of it.

Then keep three questions separate:

- **Eligible?** Do the candidate's verified qualifications plausibly meet the actual requirements? Keep each one met, unmet or unresolved, with the posting's words and the candidate's evidence side by side.
- **Practical?** Is the role acceptable on pay, location, schedule, on-call, travel, employment type, authority and the candidate's other constraints?
- **Growth?** How well do the responsibilities serve the authorized next-role objectives?

Rules:

- **Don't penalize new duties.** Not having done a duty before is normally neutral, and may be good growth. Only an actual requirement for prior experience makes it a gap. If the only reason against a role is "hasn't done this duty", remove the penalty.
- **Preferred-only criteria never reject a role.**
- **Unknown isn't met, and it isn't a gap.** If a summary doesn't show the candidate's evidence, look at the Story Bank before deciding. If it's still unclear, mark it unresolved and say what would settle it.
- **Growth never rescues an unmet hard requirement or a practical deal-breaker.**
- **The application form wins** when it reveals a stricter screen than the posting.
- **Cite both sides** for any decision that turns on a credential, education, authority or tenure: the employer's exact words, and the candidate's exact fact or claim boundary.

### The decision view

For each serious role, note in the tracker:

1. **What the employer is buying**: "They're buying ___ to solve ___."
2. **Role type**: the closest role type in `candidate-profile.md`, and at most one supporting type.
3. **Looks similar but isn't?** A title can look right while the central job buys a different person, for example a program role that's really staff-engineer depth, a transformation role that's pure strategy or communications, a PMO role that's mostly status reporting, a delivery role that's really account, revenue or contract ownership, or an M&A role that's deal finance or legal. This prompts a closer look at the requirements; it isn't a reject list.
4. **Core job**: the three or four central functions, with the candidate's evidence for each, or "new responsibility" where they haven't done it.
5. **Requirements**: required and preferred, each met, unmet or unresolved.
6. **Hiring credibility**: given the likely pool of qualified applicants, how believable is the candidate as the hire? Very High, High, Medium or Low. A reduced rating must cite an actual requirement, preference or evidenced screen, never "hasn't done these duties".
7. **Why this candidate over a likely exact-title applicant?** An evidence-based answer.
8. **Practical fit** against the candidate's constraints. Unposted pay is unknown, not a fail. Office time, travel or on-call burden should raise the bar on pay and value. A title alone isn't a reason to reject.
9. **Environment signals**. Good: strong specialists who need integration leadership, complex work that needs structure, sponsors who act on escalations, real transformation or scale-up. Worth questioning: high accountability with little authority, normalized fire drills, mostly reporting or admin, punishment for raising problems.
10. **Overall fit**: Exceptional, Very Strong, Strong, Stretch or Weak. Use the scale only where the evidence supports the distinction; "unknown" beats false precision.
11. **Posting status** (below).
12. **Decision and reason** (below).

Don't reject every weakness. Separate central gaps from credibility gaps, context gaps and learnable gaps. A stretch the candidate authorizes is valid; it doesn't turn a gap into a match. Don't let the search shrink to jobs the candidate has literally held before.

### Is the posting real and live?

Job boards, aggregators, alerts and secondary sites are leads, not proof. Record one status:

- **LIVE**: the employer's own posting, or its official applicant tracking system, is current and the application page opens;
- **OFFICIAL, APPLY NOT CHECKED**: the employer's posting is current, but you haven't reached the application page;
- **SECONDARY ONLY**: only a secondary source, current enough to evaluate;
- **UNRESOLVED**: blocked or conflicting;
- **CLOSED**: the employer has removed it;
- **TRIGGER ONLY**: an event, not a job.

Only apply through a credible employer application path. The employer's closure outranks surviving copies elsewhere. A posting's age alone isn't a bad sign; it only means check it more carefully.

### Decisions

- **SHORTLIST**: strong enough to apply.
- **WATCH / SELECTIVE STRETCH**: credible core work, but material gaps; apply selectively.
- **HOLD / VERIFY**: possibly viable; something must be checked first.
- **DEPRIORITIZE**: some fit, but not worth normal effort.
- **REJECT**: don't pursue; record the main reason.
- **CLOSED**: no longer open.
- **DUPLICATE**: already tracked.
- **TRIGGER, NO VIABLE ROLE**.

**Ranking.** Rank actionable roles by overall value, not prestige: pay, authority, scope, work arrangement, travel and on-call, career growth, the employer's signal, portability, stress and how recently the posting was confirmed. When two are close, prefer the newer or more recently confirmed one. Prestige never excuses a bad scope.

## Building a resume for a role

One model does the whole build. If your tools allow sub-agents, you may use them for parts of it, and you stay responsible for the result.

Open the posting, `candidate-profile.md`, the resume bank and the base resume before you start, and read them. Don't work from memory. Keep a short application note for the role from the layout at the end of this file, saved beside its resume.

### Step 1. Read the role

Open the posting from the employer's own site. Confirm it's the role that was chosen, that it's still open, and that it's worth applying to. If the employer shows it has closed, stop and mark it CLOSED. If you can't tell, carry on, mark the posting status UNRESOLVED, and leave the live check for the candidate review.

### Step 2. The pitch

Write, in plain words:

- what the employer is buying: "They're buying ___ to solve ___";
- the three or four central functions, the outcomes they need, and the risks they're trying to reduce;
- the required and preferred qualifications, kept separate;
- the likely hiring-manager concerns;
- useful employer vocabulary that the candidate can use truthfully;
- **the pitch**: who the candidate is for this role, the employer-level reason to believe it, and the small set of evidence that proves it. Choose one role type to lead, and at most one supporting type.

The candidate's seniority and real ownership lead. Keywords and mechanics never displace them unless the role really is tactical.

### Step 3. Match the evidence

For each material qualification, find the candidate's best evidence in the capability map and resume bank, and its claim boundary. Mark each **COVERED**, **GAP** or **DELIBERATE OMISSION**. A stretch the candidate authorized never changes a gap to covered.

For central duties the candidate hasn't done, mark them as new responsibility; don't imply past experience. If you find new evidence the bank doesn't have, add it through "Keeping the Story Bank current" in Phase 2, not as a one-off claim for this application.

### Step 4. Warm path first

Start this as soon as the role is chosen; it doesn't wait for the other steps. Ask: does the candidate know someone there, or can someone introduce them? Check People in `tracker.md`, and look further for high-priority roles.

If there's a path, draft the ask for the candidate and add a next action in People. Don't delay a time-sensitive application while waiting for a referral: apply when ready, and pursue the warm path in parallel. A warm path doesn't change the opportunity's origin.

### Step 5. Edit list

Write a plain list of the changes to make to a copy of the base resume. Nothing else changes.

- headline and summary;
- which experience leads, and in what order;
- each bullet in the tailored sections: **KEEP**, **CUT**, **REWRITE** (with the exact new text and the story it comes from) or **ADD** (with the exact text and its story);
- the exact technology or keyword lines;
- anything deliberately left alone.

Rules:

- Fixed sections don't change.
- Reuse the resume bank's approved wording unless the emphasis, the evidence or the quality calls for a change.
- No "if space permits" or "optional" items. If two versions are both good, show both and let the candidate choose.
- Don't change the base resume itself here. Note improvements for Step 9.

### Step 6. Build

Copy the base resume and apply the edit list. For each new or substantively changed bullet, run Phase 4 `RB-01` through `RB-05`, using a current applicable method brief under `RS-01` through `RS-08`. Then save it with a clear file name, for example `Firstname-Lastname-Company-Role-YYYY-MM-DD`. Then run the five checks as a preflight.

### The five checks

Run all five as a preflight before the candidate sees the draft, and record each result in the application note. Fix what fails; don't send a failing draft with a note. Two of the checks are run again just before the candidate submits: the posting check (3) always, and any other check whose inputs have changed since the preflight.

1. **No overstated claims.** Every claim and every number traces to the underlying reconstructed and approved evidence in the Story Bank or resume bank, keeping its qualifiers, scope and confidence. The base resume carries that evidence forward; it cannot be the sole proof of a claim. Confirm chronology against the candidate-checked record. No verb or object goes beyond what the candidate owned. Flag weak verbs that hide real ownership, and strengthen them only within the evidence.
2. **Required qualifications are covered or flagged.** List every material requirement and the bullet that carries it, or mark it a gap. Polished wording never hides an uncovered hard screen.
3. **The posting is still live.** In the preflight, record what the employer's site showed and when. Recheck it just before the candidate submits, in Step 8; a preflight result alone isn't enough to apply on.
4. **It's readable by screening software.** This is a formatting check: standard headings, real employer, title and date lines, no content hidden in images, text boxes or complex tables, and nothing that would read as a fake job. Record it as "formatting checked". It isn't proof of how the employer's system will read the file. Only when the candidate sees the employer's form parse the resume, and shares what it shows, is it an observed parser test: then review that parse, fix what it misread, and record "parse observed".
5. **One final read-through.** Fixed sections match the base exactly. The whole resume tells one clear story about why the candidate fits the central work, without redundant proof. House style is right. The file name and document properties are correct, with no old job titles in the metadata, no leftover comments or tracked changes, and no placeholders.

### Step 7. Candidate review

Show the candidate the resume, the pitch in two or three lines, and the gaps. They:

- approve it, or ask for changes;
- decide how to handle each gap: apply anyway, address it in an application answer, ask for a cover letter, or don't apply.

If the employer requires a cover letter, say so here, and ask whether the candidate wants you to draft one. Write it only if they say yes (see "Cover letters, only on request").

You don't submit anything. The candidate does, in Step 8.

### Step 8. Close out

The candidate submits. You help prepare the application, but you never create an account on an employer's or job site, and you never submit or try to submit an application, even if the candidate asks. The candidate does both.

1. Just before they submit, recheck that the posting is live (check 3), and rerun any check whose inputs changed since the preflight. If the employer shows it has closed, tell the candidate not to submit, and mark it CLOSED.
2. If the candidate shares how the employer's form read the resume, review that parse (check 4) and fix what it misread.
3. Wait for the candidate to confirm the application went in, or for the employer's confirmation. Until then, the role stays in Opportunities with work status "ready to apply".

Once submission is confirmed:

- move the role from Opportunities to Applications in `tracker.md`, with the date and the resume file used;
- save the final submitted copy and the posting text, or a link to it, beside the application note;
- note any application-form details worth remembering in the tracker row.

**Completion contract:** scope is `APPLICATION:<id>:PREPARATION`, not the whole search. `P5-G01` passes only when all five checks above pass on the current materials, including any required `RB`/`RS` work; `P5-G02` passes only with candidate approval of those materials and the gap handling. Both must PASS before preparation is COMPLETE under the shared transition contract. `SUBMITTED` additionally requires the Step 8 candidate/employer confirmation; preparation completion cannot supply it. Keep prepared, approved, ready to apply and submitted distinct. Starting an interview does not complete the wider search.

### Step 9. Improve the base, through the journal

Write in the journal anything the build showed about the base resume or the Story Bank: a stronger bullet worth keeping, a story that's missing, a recurring requirement the base doesn't cover. The next consolidation round picks these up. With the candidate's approval, a change worth keeping goes into the next version of the base resume as one of:

- **PROMOTE TO THE NEXT BASE VERSION**;
- **DEFER**;
- **REJECT AS ROLE-SPECIFIC**;
- **NO CHANGE**.

### Running the build overnight

The build is a good fit for a scheduled job: the candidate picks roles in the evening and wakes to drafts ready for review.

- If you have a tool for scheduled tasks, offer it: "Want me to build these overnight so they're ready in the morning?"
- If you're not sure, ask the candidate whether their app can schedule a task.
- If neither, build in the conversation.

A scheduled build runs Steps 1 to 6 and the five checks as a preflight, saves the drafts and the application notes with the preflight results, and writes a journal entry. It stops before Step 7: nothing is submitted, no cover letter is written unless the candidate already asked for one, and no message goes to anyone. If a check fails and can't be fixed from the files, it notes why and leaves that draft for the candidate. The posting check is repeated before submission in Step 8, whatever the overnight run found.

## Cover letters, only on request

Write a cover letter only when the candidate asks for one. If the employer requires a letter, tell the candidate and ask whether they want you to draft it; don't draft it unasked.

- **Required by the employer, and the candidate asked for a draft**: answer what the employer asks, in the form they ask for.
- **Optional**: a letter must earn its place. Ask: if the employer never saw it, what useful understanding would disappear? If the answer is "nothing the resume doesn't already say", don't write one, and say why.
- The letter isn't a prose resume or a declaration of enthusiasm. It connects the candidate's evidence to the employer's real need: transferability, context, judgment or an approved perspective.
- Every factual sentence traces to the Story Bank, under the same rules as the resume. A personal belief or motivation in the letter must be one the candidate has actually stated.
- One page by default, unless the employer says otherwise. Use a verified addressee or a plain generic greeting; never guess a name.
- Mention a gap only when it clarifies honestly; name the specific boundary and the transfer, without implying the gap is gone.
- Plain, direct language the candidate could use in a thoughtful conversation. Don't use AI detectors or banned-word lists as quality checks.
- The candidate approves it before it goes.

## Tracking applications

Application stages: **Applied**, **Screening / Assessment**, **First interview**, **Second interview**, **Later / final interview**, **Offer**, **Rejected** (only when the employer says so), **Role closed**, **Withdrawn**, **No response**.

How long silence means:

| Days since applying, with no meaningful contact | Treat as |
|---|---|
| 0 to 21 | Active; silence means nothing yet |
| 22 to 30 | Aging; contact still plausible |
| 31 to 44 | Low-probability, but still active |
| 45 or more | No response, for counting purposes. This isn't a rejection |

Extend these when the employer said the process is long, the portal shows movement, or the employer is known to be slow.

When an interview is scheduled, start Phase 6 for that role.

## The regular review

About once a month, or every fourth run of a search angle, or when the candidate asks:

- **By origin.** For relationships, recruiters and cold applications separately: how many opportunities, applications, replies, screens, interviews and offers each produced. This shows where the candidate's time pays off.
- **By direction.** For each role family or hiring thesis: roles found, roles that passed evaluation, applications, responses and outcomes.
- **Search angles.** A search angle that comes back empty four runs in a row gets a retarget review. Eight empty runs is strong evidence to retire it, but the candidate decides.
- **Market learning.** Repeated requirements, repeated credibility screens and new search vocabulary. If the market picture has changed, refresh the affected parts of Phase 3. One unusual job doesn't redefine the market, and weak response alone doesn't prove a direction wrong.

Write the review as a journal entry, and propose changes for the candidate to decide.
